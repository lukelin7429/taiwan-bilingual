Across Taiwan · 各縣市

Every school carries its own town

The hub grows county by county. Each campus learns English through its own town's landmarks, food and stories — so bilingual learning stays rooted in a real place.

資源網一個縣市一個縣市地成長。每一所學校都透過自己城鎮的地標、美食與故事學英語——讓雙語學習扎根在真實的土地上。

County 01

Chiayi嘉義縣

From Alishan's sunrise to the Palace Museum's Southern Branch
從阿里山的雲海日出,到嘉南平原的故宮南院

Alishan阿里山

Sunrise, seas of cloud, thousand-year sacred trees and a century-old forest railway — four distinct seasons above 2,000 metres.

Palace Museum · Southern Branch故宮南院

In Taibao City — the "Museum of Asian Art and Culture," beside the high-speed rail, with hands-on children's programs.

Taibao City太保市

The county seat and HSR district — and the hometown of the hub's founding teacher.

Turkey rice & KANO火雞肉飯・KANO 棒球

Chiayi's signature turkey rice, high-mountain tea, and a proud baseball history.

County 02

Taichung臺中市

Central Taiwan's metropolis — architecture, art and coastal wetlands
中台灣核心都會——建築、藝術與海岸生態並陳

National Taichung Theater國家歌劇院

Toyo Ito's "sound cave" — a one-of-a-kind curved-wall building with no straight 90° walls anywhere inside.

Gaomei Wetlands高美濕地

1,500 hectares at the Dajia River mouth — wind turbines, a famous orange sunset, and a model boardwalk for eco-conservation.

City life都會生活

Fengjia Night Market, Miyahara, and the birthplace of bubble tea — everyday Taiwanese city culture.

Shengang & Qingshui神岡・清水

Village and coastal communities where the hub's immersive-English campuses sit.

County 03

Changhua彰化縣

Taiwan's rice barn and a county of heritage towns
台灣的米倉與古蹟之鄉

Baguashan Great Buddha八卦山大佛

Changhua City's landmark — a towering Buddha on Bagua Mountain, with hiking and cycling trails along the ridge.

Lukang Old Town鹿港古鎮

A Qing-era trading port — Longshan Temple ("Taiwan's Forbidden City"), the Mazu Temple, and old-street snacks.

Fan-shaped Roundhouse扇形車庫

Taiwan's only surviving fan-shaped train depot, with its locomotive turntable — a railfan pilgrimage.

Food & literature美食與文學

Changhua meatballs, Lukang oyster omelette — and the writer Lai He, "father of Taiwanese new literature."
