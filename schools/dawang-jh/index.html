Dawang Junior High School

大王國中

The junior high of "the township where the sun rises first" — a small coastal school where most students are Paiwan Indigenous.

About · 學校簡介

A regional hub for two townships.

Dawang Junior High was established in 1968 as Taiwan rolled out nine-year compulsory education nationwide. It now draws students from five elementary schools across both Taimali and Jinfeng Townships, making it the shared junior high for a wide stretch of Taitung's southern coast. With roughly 166 students, the school runs six regular classes alongside three Indigenous experimental classes and one special-education class — a structure that reflects its student body, of which about 86 percent are Indigenous, predominantly Paiwan.

大王國中創立於民國57年（1968年），正值台灣全面推行九年國民義務教育之際。學校招收來自太麻里與金峰兩鄉、五所國小的畢業生，是這片台東南部海岸地區共同的國中。全校約166名學生，設有6個一般班、3個原住民實驗班及1個特教班，原住民學生比例約達86%，以排灣族為主。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🌅 Named for "The Great King"

校名源自大王村

The school takes its name from Dawang Village, itself named for a historic Paiwan chief once called "the Great King" — a namesake that anchors the school firmly in local Paiwan history.

🏫 A Shared School for Two Townships

跨鄉服務的國中

Students arrive from five different elementary schools spanning both Taimali and Jinfeng Townships, making Dawang Junior High a genuine regional hub along the South-Link coast.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Indigenous Experimental Classes

原住民實驗班

Alongside its regular classes, the school runs three Indigenous experimental classes designed around the needs and culture of its majority-Paiwan student body.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Taimali, where Taiwan first sees the sun.

🌄

Taimali Township is popularly known across Taiwan as the place where the sun rises first each day — a coastal community along the South-Link Highway with a strong Paiwan and Amis Indigenous presence, of which Dawang Village forms the administrative heart.
