About · 學校簡介

A modern campus with a name that echoes the whole city.

New Taipei Senior High School opened in 1997 as Taiwan Provincial Sanchong Senior High School, with some sources citing an earlier 1994 preparatory-office start. It became a national school under the name National Sanchong Senior High School in 2000, and was renamed New Taipei Senior High School in 2013, after Taipei County's 2010 upgrade to New Taipei City status. Set on a modern campus of roughly 7.6 hectares, the school enrolls about 1,800 to 1,900 students across 51 classes, including specialty music and PE classes. Its facilities include an indoor heated swimming pool, a multipurpose gymnasium, a planetarium and observatory, an international conference hall, a digital library, and a concert hall, and it maintains sister-school relationships with institutions in Japan and Germany.

新北高中創立於民國86年（1997年），初名「臺灣省立三重高級中學」，亦有資料指出其前身可追溯至民國83年（1994年）籌備處成立。民國89年（2000年）改為國立，稱「國立三重高中」；民國102年（2013年）配合臺北縣升格新北市，更名為「新北高級中學」。校地約7.6公頃，現有學生約1,800至1,900人，共51班，並設有音樂班與體育班。校內設施包括室內溫水游泳池、多功能體育館、天文台與觀測站、國際會議廳、數位圖書館及音樂廳，並與日本、德國學校締結姊妹校關係。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🏊 A Heated Indoor Pool

室內溫水游泳池

The 7.6-hectare campus includes an indoor heated swimming pool and a multipurpose gymnasium — facilities well beyond what most senior highs offer.

🔭 A Planetarium and Observatory

天文台與觀測站

An on-campus planetarium and observatory give students hands-on access to astronomy, alongside an international conference hall and digital library.

🎵 Music and PE Specialty Classes

音樂班與體育班

Dedicated music and physical-education classes run alongside general academic tracks, backed by a dedicated concert hall.

🌏 Sister Schools Abroad

日本、德國姊妹校

The school maintains sister-school ties with institutions in Japan and Germany, giving students access to international exchange opportunities.

Local Culture · 在地文化

A name born from a city's upgrade.

🏙️

The school's name is often confused with the whole city — "New Taipei High School" vs. New Taipei City — a quirk stemming from Sanchong/Taipei County's 2010 upgrade to New Taipei City status, reflecting the district's shift from rural county town to metropolis.
