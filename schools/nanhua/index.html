About · 學校簡介

Founded by Fo Guang Shan, and built for record-setting growth.

Nanhua University began as Nanhua College of Management, approved in April 1996 and opened on August 1, 1996, by Venerable Master Hsing Yun (星雲大師) — founder of Fo Guang Shan, one of the major organizations of Humanistic Buddhism. It was the first comprehensive, multidisciplinary university founded by Fo Guang Shan. Just three years later, in August 1999, it was upgraded to full university status — at the time, the shortest span from college to university recognized in Taiwan. Set on a forested campus in Dalin Township accommodating over 2,000 resident students, Nanhua today enrolls roughly 5,500 students and maintains partnerships with more than 100 universities across 30-plus countries.

南華大學前身為南華管理學院，於民國85年（1996年）4月獲准設立，同年8月1日由佛光山開山宗長星雲大師主持開學典禮。這是佛光山創辦的第一所綜合型大學。僅僅三年後，民國88年（1999年）8月1日即改制為大學，是當時台灣由學院升格為大學所需時間最短的紀錄。校園座落於大林鎮一片林木蓊鬱的土地上，可容納兩千餘名學生住宿，目前全校學生約5,500人，並與逾30個國家、100多所大學建立合作關係。

Academics · 學術特色

What makes this university worth knowing.

🕊️ Pioneer of Life-and-Death Studies

生死學研究先驅

Nanhua established a Graduate Institute of Life-and-Death Studies (生死學研究所) in 1997 — among the first dedicated academic programs on the subject anywhere in the world.

🌏 Genuine English Website

實質英文官網

Nanhua's English site (en3.nhu.edu.tw) is a real, actively maintained portal with admissions information, campus life, and news for international, mainland, and overseas-Chinese students.

🌳 A Green, Forested Campus

綠意盎然的生態校園

Nanhua's campus is known for its eco-friendly, forested setting, with dormitories accommodating more than 2,000 students living on campus.

🤝 100+ International Partnerships

逾百所海外夥伴大學

Nanhua maintains partnerships with more than 100 universities in over 30 countries, supporting active international and cross-strait exchange programs.

Local Culture · 在地文化

A cornerstone of Humanistic Buddhism education in Chiayi.

🙏

Nanhua University is directly part of the Fo Guang Shan Buddhist lineage founded by Master Hsing Yun, making it a cornerstone of Dalin Township's identity as a center of Humanistic Buddhism education and practice — a distinct strand of Chiayi County's cultural life alongside the region's agricultural heritage.
