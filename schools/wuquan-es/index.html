About · 學校簡介

Named for a Republican-era political ideal.

Wuquan Elementary split off from the Japanese-era Qingye National School on April 1, 1942, and was renamed Wuquan National School in September 1946. Its name, "五權" ("five powers"), references Sun Yat-sen's Five-Power Constitution, a common naming convention from Taiwan's early Republican era. In 1984, the school added a special class for hearing-impaired students.

五權國小於1942年（昭和17年，日治時期）4月1日自「青葉國民學校」分出設校，1946年9月更名為五權國民學校。校名「五權」取自孫中山「五權憲法」理念，是台灣早期民國時代常見的命名傳統。1984年，學校增設聽障特殊班。

🏛️ Named for Sun Yat-sen's Five Powers

五權憲法命名傳統

"五權" references Sun Yat-sen's Five-Power Constitution, one of many Republican-era school names that carried political philosophy into everyday place names.

🎌 Split Off During Japanese Rule

日治末期自青葉分出

The school began in 1942 as a split from the Japanese-era Qingye National School, only receiving its current name after the war, in 1946.

🤟 A Special Class Since 1984

1984年增設聽障特殊班

The school added a dedicated class for hearing-impaired students in 1984, reflecting an early commitment to inclusive education.
