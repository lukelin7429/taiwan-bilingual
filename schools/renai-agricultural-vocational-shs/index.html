About · 學校簡介

The highest school in Taiwan.

National Ren-Ai Agricultural Vocational Senior High School was founded in 1932 under Japanese colonial rule as the "Taichu Prefecture Wushe Agricultural Training Institute," established near Wushe in the wake of Japanese agricultural policy in the region. Renamed multiple times over the following decades, it became a national school in February 2000. Sitting at roughly 1,150 meters above sea level, it is the highest-altitude senior high school in Taiwan. The school is designated a key indigenous-education institution, with the majority of its students Indigenous.

國立仁愛高級農業職業學校創校於1932年（昭和7年），日治時期名為「台中州霧社農業專修學校」，設立於霧社附近，反映當時日本殖民政府的農業政策。歷經多次更名，於2000年2月改制為國立學校。校址海拔約1,150公尺，是全台灣海拔最高的高級中學。學校為重點原住民教育學校，多數學生為原住民族。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

⛰️ Taiwan's Highest-Altitude Senior High

全台海拔最高的高中

Sitting at roughly 1,150 meters above sea level, the campus holds the distinction of being the highest-altitude senior high school anywhere in Taiwan.

🌾 Roots in Agricultural Training

農業專修學校的起源

Founded in 1932 as the Wushe Agricultural Training Institute under Japanese rule, the school's origins are tied directly to colonial-era agricultural education policy in the mountains.

🎓 A Key Indigenous-Education School

重點原住民教育學校

Designated a key indigenous-education school with a majority Indigenous student body, it plays a central role in secondary and vocational education for Ren'ai Township's mountain communities.

🏛️ National School Since 2000

2000年改制國立學校

After multiple renamings through the postwar era, the school became a national institution in February 2000, marking its current chapter as National Ren-Ai Agricultural Vocational Senior High School.

Local Culture · 在地文化

In the heart of Seediq homeland, near Wushe.

⛰️

Founded near Wushe in the aftermath of Japanese colonial agricultural policy, the school stands in the heart of Ren'ai Township's mountainous Seediq homeland, near the site of the 1930 Wushe Incident — a pivotal and painful chapter of Indigenous resistance to Japanese rule that continues to shape the region's identity and memory today.
