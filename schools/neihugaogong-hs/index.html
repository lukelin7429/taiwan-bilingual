About · 學校簡介

Training technicians for the automation era.

Taipei Municipal Neihu Vocational High School traces its start to a preparatory office established in 1983, with its first students enrolling in 1986. It is considered Taiwan's first vocational high school built specifically around an electrical/electronics department cluster, with founding departments in Information, Electronics, Electrical Engineering, Control Engineering, and Refrigeration & Air Conditioning — a curriculum designed explicitly to supply skilled technical labor as Taiwan pushed into industrial automation. Over the following decades the school broadened its offerings, adding a comprehensive vocational department (later renamed Retail Services) in 2002, a physical-education special class in 2007, and an Applied English department in 2013.

內湖高工的籌備處成立於民國72年（1983年），並於民國75年（1986年）首度招生。學校被視為全臺第一所以電機電子群職科為核心規劃的高級工業職業學校，創校科別包括資訊科、電子科、電機科、控制科與冷凍空調科，課程設計直接對應台灣邁向工業自動化所需的技術人力。此後數十年間，學校持續拓展科別，民國91年（2002年）增設綜合職能科（後更名為服務業經營科），民國96年（2007年）成立體育特色班，民國102年（2013年）再增設應用英語科。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

⚡ Taiwan's First Electronics-Cluster School

全臺首創電機電子群

The school is considered Taiwan's first vocational high school organized around an electrical/electronics department cluster, founded with programs in Information, Electronics, Electrical Engineering, Control Engineering, and Refrigeration & Air Conditioning.

📈 Decades of Expanding Programs

科別持續擴展

Beyond its founding technical departments, the school later added a comprehensive vocational/Retail Services department (2002), a PE special class (2007), and an Applied English department (2013).

🏭 Built for the Automation Era

因應自動化而生

The school was established specifically to train the skilled technical labor Taiwan needed as its industries pushed into automation, giving it a direct link to the country's industrial development.

Local Culture · 在地文化

A founding mission that foreshadowed Neihu's tech future.

💡

Neihu Vocational High's founding mission — supplying skilled technical workers for Taiwan's automation-era industry — foreshadowed the district's later transformation into Taipei's largest technology-park district. Decades before Neihu Technology Park became one of Taiwan's major tech hubs, this school was already training the electronics and electrical technicians the region would come to depend on.
