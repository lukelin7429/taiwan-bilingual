Tainan Municipal Wenxian Junior High School

文賢國中

Opened in 1987 to serve North District's growing Wenxian/Wencheng neighborhood — not to be confused with the unrelated school of the same name in Rende District.

About · 學校簡介

Built to serve a growing 1980s neighborhood.

Wenxian Junior High School (North District) opened its doors on August 1, 1987. Planning began in 1984, when the city instructed Dexi Junior High to prepare a founding budget; Zhang Lingcai, then principal of Jincheng Junior High, was appointed in 1985 to head the preparatory office, and construction began in November 1986. The campus site was chosen in North District's Wenxian/Wencheng neighborhood. Note: there is a separate, unrelated school also named "文賢國中" in Tainan's Rende District — this profile covers specifically the North District school, whose official site is whjh.tn.edu.tw.

文賢國中（北區）於1987年（民國76年）8月1日正式開校。籌設作業始於1984年（民國73年），市府指示德智國中編列創校預算；1985年（民國74年）由時任進學國中校長張齡才受命主持籌備處，1986年11月動工興建。校地選定於北區文賢／文成一帶。特別說明：臺南市仁德區另有一所同名「文賢國中」，與本校無關，本頁專指北區這所學校，官網為whjh.tn.edu.tw。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🏗️ A Three-Year Planning Process

三年籌備期

From the 1984 budget directive to the 1987 opening, Wenxian's founding took roughly three years of planning, land selection, and construction.

🏘️ Named for Its Neighborhood

以文賢／文成社區命名

The school takes its name from the Wenxian/Wencheng neighborhood in North District where its campus was built to serve residential growth inland from the district's older core.

🆕 One of North District's Newer Junior Highs

北區較新設立的國中之一

Founded in 1987, Wenxian is one of North District's newer secondary schools, built specifically to serve 1980s residential expansion.

⚠️ Not to Be Confused with Its Rende Namesake

與仁德區同名學校無關

Tainan has a second, unrelated school also called 文賢國中 in Rende District — this school, at whjh.tn.edu.tw, is specifically the North District campus.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Serving 1980s growth inland from the old core.

🏘️

Wenxian Junior High is one of North District's newer (1980s) junior highs, built to serve residential growth inland from the district's historic railway and temple core — part of the same wave of growth that produced several of North District's newer public schools in that era.
