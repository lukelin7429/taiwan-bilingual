Liouguei Senior High School

高雄市立六龜高級中學（含附屬國民中學）

The only complete school in the greater Qishan/Liouguei mountain region, spun off from Meinong to serve the remote Laonong River valley.

About · 學校簡介

One school, two divisions, from Meinong to the mountains.

Liouguei Senior High School traces its roots to September 1956, when it opened as a branch of Meinong Middle School (高雄縣立美濃中學六龜分部), founded to serve the more remote, mountainous Liouguei area along the Laonong River, apart from Meinong's Hakka cultural center. It became independent in 1958 as Kaohsiung County Liouguei Junior High School, was renamed Kaohsiung County Liouguei National Junior High School in 1968, and was upgraded to a complete school spanning junior and senior high divisions in 2000. Note: this school appears twice in some directories — once as 高雄市立六龜高級中學 (senior high) and once as 六龜高級中學附屬國民中學 (its junior-high division) — because it is a single institution, with one principal and one campus, under one administration since 2000. It became a Kaohsiung City school after the 2010 county-city merger. As of 2014 the school enrolled around 409 students on a campus of about 2.98 hectares, and it maintains an Indigenous rugby team, a choral group, and a sister-school relationship with an agricultural high school in Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan.

六龜高中創校於民國45年（1956年）9月，原為「高雄縣立美濃中學六龜分部」，為服務荖濃溪流域較為偏遠的六龜山區而自美濃（客家文化重鎮）分出設立。民國47年（1958年）獨立設校，改名「高雄縣立六龜初級中學」；民國57年（1968年）更名「高雄縣立六龜國民中學」；民國89年（2000年）改制為涵蓋國中、高中部的完全中學。提醒：本校在部分名錄中會分別以「高雄市立六龜高級中學」與「六龜高級中學附屬國民中學」兩筆資料列出，實為同一所學校——單一校長、單一校區，自2000年起同一行政體制下同時開設國中部與高中部。2010年高雄縣市合併後改為市立學校。截至2014年，學校約有409名學生，校地約2.98公頃，並設有原住民橄欖球隊、合唱團，並與日本熊本縣一所農業高中締結姊妹校。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🏔️ The Only Complete School in the Region

全區唯一完全中學

Liouguei Senior High is the only complete school (junior and senior high) in the greater Qishan/Liouguei mountain region, upgraded to that status in 2000.

🌱 Spun Off From Meinong's Hakka Heartland

自美濃分出設校

Originally a 1956 branch of Meinong Middle School — a center of Hakka culture — the school was founded to serve the more remote, mountainous Liouguei area along the Laonong River.

📛 One School, Not Two

同一所學校非兩校

Though it may appear listed separately as a senior high and a junior high, the school has been a single institution with one principal and one campus since being upgraded to complete-school status in 2000.

🏉 An Indigenous Rugby Team and a Japanese Sister School

原民橄欖球隊與日本姊妹校

The school maintains an Indigenous rugby team and a choral group, alongside a sister-school relationship with an agricultural high school in Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan.
