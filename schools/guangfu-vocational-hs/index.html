About · 學校簡介

A vocational anchor for Guangfu Township.

National Kuangfu Senior Commercial and Industrial Vocational High School opened its doors on July 1, 1996, after being approved for planning in 1991. It began with programs in data processing, accounting, auto repair, and electronics, and was elevated to national status in February 2000. The school briefly operated a comprehensive high school track between 2000 and 2014 before returning fully to vocational programs, and in 2022 it launched a bilingual culinary program — reflecting Taiwan's push to bring English instruction into technical and vocational education.

國立光復高級商工職業學校於民國85年（1996年）7月1日創校，早在民國80年（1991年）即獲准籌設。創校初期設有資料處理、會計、汽車修護、電子等科別，並於民國89年（2000年）2月改制為國立學校。學校曾於2000年至2014年間試辦綜合高中學程，後恢復為職業類科為主。民國111年（2022年）更增設雙語餐飲科，呼應台灣技職教育雙語化的推動方向。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🍳 A New Bilingual Culinary Program

雙語餐飲科

Approved in 2022, the school's bilingual culinary program trains students to cook and communicate in English — part of a broader shift toward bilingual technical education in Taiwan.

🔧 Trades That Serve the Region

扎根地方的職業科別

From auto repair and electronics to accounting and data processing, the school's programs are designed to train local youth for the sugar-refinery-tourism and agricultural economy of Guangfu Township.

🏊 Full Athletic Facilities

完整運動設施

The campus includes a 50-meter swimming pool, a 400-meter track, and a gymnasium, giving students in this rural township access to facilities not always found at small vocational schools.

🎓 A Path to National Status

從籌設到國立

The school moved from provincial approval in 1991 to full national status by 2000, growing steadily while adapting its curriculum — including a stint as a comprehensive high school — to meet local needs.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Training youth for Guangfu's Amis-centered economy.

🏭

As the only vocational senior high in Guangfu Township, home to a large Amis Indigenous population and the historic Hualien Sugar Factory, the school plays a distinctive role in preparing local — often Amis — youth for careers connected to the region's sugar-refinery-tourism and agricultural industries, rather than requiring them to leave the township for vocational training.
