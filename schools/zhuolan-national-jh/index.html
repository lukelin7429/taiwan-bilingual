National Zhuolan Senior High School, Junior High Division

國立卓蘭高中附設國中

The junior-high division of a National-level school on the Miaoli-Taichung border, serving families from Zhuolan's Hakka, Hoklo, and mountain-fruit-farming communities.

About · 學校簡介

The junior-high wing of a National-level school.

This school is the junior-high division attached to National Zhuolan Senior High School (國立卓蘭高級中等學校), a National-level (國立) institution rather than a county (縣立) school. Its roots trace back to 1952, when it was established as the Zhuolan Branch of Dahu Junior High School; the parent senior high school itself became a National school in 2000. Today the junior high division serves about 240 students across 11 classes, out of roughly 1,101 students total when combined with the senior high, and the school also maintains special education classes.

本校為國立卓蘭高級中等學校附設之國中部，屬國立層級學校，而非縣立學校。其歷史可追溯至民國41年（1952年）成立的「大湖初級中學卓蘭分校」，母體高中則於民國89年（2000年）改制為國立。目前國中部約有240名學生、11個班級，與高中部合計全校約1,101名學生，並設有特殊教育班。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🎓 A National-Level School

國立層級學校

Unlike most junior highs in the area, this is the junior-high division of National Zhuolan Senior High School — a National-level (國立), not county-level, institution.

📜 From Branch School to National Status

從分校到國立化

Founded in 1952 as the Zhuolan Branch of Dahu Junior High School, the parent institution was elevated to National status in 2000, a milestone in its history.

🏫 A Community of About 240 Students

約240名學生的國中部

The junior high division serves roughly 240 students in 11 classes, part of a combined campus of about 1,101 students alongside the senior high program.

🌱 Special Education Classes

特殊教育班

The school also maintains special education classes, extending its educational mission to students with a broader range of learning needs.

Local Culture · 在地文化

On the Miaoli-Taichung fruit-farming border.

🍐

Zhuolan sits on the Miaoli-Taichung border where Hakka and Hoklo communities have mixed for generations, and the township's terraced hillsides are famous for high-mountain fruit farming — especially pears — a livelihood many of the school's students' families depend on.
