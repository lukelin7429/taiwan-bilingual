Gongguan Elementary School

公館國民小學

The township's namesake and oldest school, founded in 1901, now its largest, with a new campus that echoes traditional Hakka courtyard architecture.

About · 學校簡介

Gongguan's founding school, still growing after 120 years.

Gongguan Elementary opened in 1901 as "Gongguan Public School," making it the oldest school in the township that shares its name. It celebrated its 120th anniversary in 2021 with a new school building, and today serves about 874 students — the largest enrollment of any school in Gongguan. The school fields a strong Chinese orchestra and music-class program, along with bridge, track-and-field, and basketball teams, and its new campus deliberately echoes the traditional Hakka sanheyuan (three-sided courtyard) architecture of the region.

公館國小創立於明治34年（1901年），初名「公館公學校」，是以公館鄉命名的最早學校。民國110年（2021年）欣逢創校120週年，並落成新校舍，目前全校約874名學生，為公館鄉規模最大的學校。學校擁有實力堅強的國樂團與音樂班，並設有橋藝、田徑、籃球等隊伍，新校舍更刻意呼應在地客家三合院的傳統建築風格。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🏛️ Architecture Rooted in Hakka Tradition

呼應客家三合院建築

The school's new campus, completed around its 120th anniversary in 2021, was designed to echo the traditional Hakka sanheyuan courtyard layout found throughout the region.

🎻 A Strong Music and Orchestra Program

國樂團與音樂班

Gongguan Elementary is known for a strong Chinese orchestra and dedicated music-class program, giving students deep, sustained exposure to musical training.

🏫 Gongguan's Largest School

全鄉規模最大

With about 874 students, Gongguan Elementary is the biggest school in the township, reflecting its role as the area's founding and namesake school.

🏃 Bridge, Track, and Basketball

橋藝、田徑、籃球隊

Beyond academics and music, the school fields competitive bridge, track-and-field, and basketball teams, giving students a broad range of extracurricular pursuits.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Gongguan's namesake, rooted in Hakka heritage.

🏠

As the township's namesake and oldest school (est. 1901), Gongguan Elementary embodies the Hakka farming and craft heritage of Gongguan — famous for rush/rattan weaving and historic camphor production — and its new campus explicitly draws on traditional Hakka courtyard architecture.
