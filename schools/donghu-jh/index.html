Taipei Municipal Donghu Junior High School

東湖國民中學

Home to a boys' choir that has been a longstanding powerhouse at Taiwan's National Student Music Competition, in the flood-prone Donghu basin along the Keelung River.

About · 學校簡介

A choir powerhouse in the East Lake basin.

Taipei Municipal Donghu Junior High School was established in the late 1980s, with sources generally citing 1989 as its founding year (though a secondary source gives 1990). The school is best known for its boys' choir (男聲合唱團), a longstanding powerhouse in Taiwan's National Student Music Competition — a 2015 news report noted a 23-consecutive-year winning streak in the Northern Region finals. As of August 2024, the school's seventh principal, Lin Kai-chiung, leads the campus, which sits in Donghu, a low-lying basin along the Keelung River.

東湖國中創立於1980年代末，多數資料採民國78年（1989年）為創校年（另有資料記載為民國79年／1990年）。學校最為人稱道的是男聲合唱團，長年為全國學生音樂比賽的勁旅——據2015年新聞報導，該合唱團在北區決賽已締造連續23年獲獎的紀錄。現任第七任校長林凱瓊自民國113年（2024年）8月接任，校址位於基隆河畔的東湖盆地。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🎤 A 23-Year Choir Winning Streak

合唱團連23年獲獎

The school's boys' choir has been a longstanding powerhouse at Taiwan's National Student Music Competition, with a 2015 report noting a 23-consecutive-year winning streak in the Northern Region finals.

👤 Seven Generations of Leadership

歷經七任校長

Since its founding, the school has been led by seven principals, with Lin Kai-chiung taking office as the seventh in August 2024.

🌊 Built on Reclaimed Flood-Basin Land

建於昔日淹水盆地

The school sits in Donghu, a low-lying basin along the Keelung River that was prone to flooding until flood-control works allowed its dense residential development from the 1980s onward.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Named for a lake that no longer exists.

🌊

Donghu Junior High is located in Donghu (East Lake), a low-lying basin along the Keelung River that was prone to flooding until flood-control works enabled its dense residential build-out in the 1980s and '90s — the "lake" in the name refers to this historically wet terrain, not an actual lake that exists today.
