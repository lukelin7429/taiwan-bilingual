Lili Elementary School

About · 學校簡介

A century-old school serving three Paiwan communities.

Lili Elementary School was founded in 1911, during the Japanese colonial era, and its catchment area today spans three Paiwan tribal communities: Lili, Guichong, and Qijia. With a predominantly Paiwan student body, the school promotes ethnic and indigenous education alongside its academic program, and is especially known for its traditional dance troupe, which won an “excellent” award at a national dance competition, and its soccer team, a regular top-three finisher in county competitions.

力里國小創校於明治44年（1911年）日治時期，學區涵蓋力里、歸崇、七佳三個排灣族部落。學生以排灣族為主，學校積極推動民族教育與學生特色發展，其中傳統舞蹈隊曾於110學年度全國舞蹈競賽榮獲優等，足球隊亦經常在縣內比賽中名列前三。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

🪨 The Laochijia Slate-House Village

老七佳石板屋聚落

The Qijia community in the school's catchment is home to Laochijia (老七佳), one of Taiwan's best-preserved traditional Paiwan slate-house villages — registered as Pingtung County cultural heritage in 2010 and a potential World Heritage site since 2013.

💃 An Award-Winning Dance Troupe

全國優等舞蹈隊

The school's traditional Paiwan dance troupe won an “excellent” award at a national dance competition in the 110th school year, keeping ceremonial dance traditions alive for a new generation.

⚽ A Competitive Soccer Team

縣賽常勝軍

Lili Elementary's soccer team regularly places in the top three at county-level competitions, a source of pride for this small mountain community.

🏔️ Deep Paiwan Mountain Roots

深厚排灣山林淵源

Local Culture · 在地文化

Chunri, land of the Laochijia slate-house village.

🪨

Chunri Township is a mountainous Paiwan indigenous township, and the Lili tribal community served by this school was historically among the most powerful Paiwan groups, with traditional territory once extending from the Jinshuiying Historic Trail (浸水營古道) toward Taitung. Its catchment includes the Qijia community, home to the Laochijia (老七佳) slate-house settlement — one of the best-preserved traditional Paiwan slate-house (石板屋) villages in Taiwan, where ceremonies such as the harvest festival and the five-year ancestral-spirit ritual (maljeveq) are still practiced today.
