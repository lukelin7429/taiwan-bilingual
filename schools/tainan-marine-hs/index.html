About · 學校簡介

A national school with a maritime mission.

Tainan Marine Vocational School traces its roots to August 1946, when it opened in Kaohsiung as a branch of the Keelung Fishery Vocational School, using a former Japanese shipyard as a practice shipbuilding facility. It became an independent school in 1960 under the name "Taiwan Provincial Tainan Senior Fishery Vocational School" and moved to Tainan, taking its current national title in 2000 after Taiwan's provincial-government reorganization. Today the school offers technical tracks in aquaculture, food processing, marine navigation and engine operations, electronics, and digital commerce, and launched an AI Robot Excellence Program in 2019.

臺南海事水產學校的校史可追溯至1946年（民國35年）8月，最初於高雄設立，作為基隆水產職業學校的分校，借用日治時期造船廠作為實習造船場地。1960年（民國49年）獨立設校，遷至臺南，定名為「臺灣省立臺南水產職業學校」；2000年精省後改為現今的國立校名。學校現設有水產養殖、食品加工、航海、輪機、電機與數位商務等專業科別，並於2019年成立「AI機器人卓越班」。

Highlights · 學校特色

What makes this school worth knowing.

⚓ Rooted in Shipbuilding History

源自造船廠的實習傳統

The school began in 1946 on the site of a former Japanese shipyard in Kaohsiung, using it as a practice shipbuilding facility before relocating to Tainan in 1960.

🐟 One of Taiwan's Few Fishery Vocational Schools

全台少見的水產海事高職

A dedicated marine and fishery curriculum — aquaculture, food processing, navigation and marine engineering — reflects Anping's centuries-old identity as a fishing and shipping port.

🤖 AI Robot Excellence Program

AI機器人卓越班

Launched in 2019, the program adds modern robotics and AI training alongside the school's traditional maritime and technical tracks.

🧭 School Motto: Sincerity, Respect, Diligence, Simplicity

校訓：誠敬勤樸

The school's four-character motto (誠敬勤樸) sets the tone for a disciplined, hands-on technical education.

Local Culture · 在地文化

Anping's working harbor, in the classroom.

⚓

Sitting a few minutes from Anping Harbor, this national maritime and fishery school directly continues Anping's centuries-old identity as Taiwan's oldest trading port and fishing community. Its students train in the same waters the Dutch East India Company once sailed into to build Fort Zeelandia — a living link between Anping's colonial-era port history and its present-day fishing and aquaculture industry.
